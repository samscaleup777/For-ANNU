Women’s safety

Prepared, not fearful.

Safety is not about living in fear. It is about practical habits, clear boundaries, good digital hygiene and knowing what to do when a situation changes.

Calm preparation matters.

Keep useful information accessible, trust your judgment and make a plan before you need one.

Safety foundations

Four areas worth preparing for

Small preventive steps can make everyday situations easier to manage without creating unnecessary anxiety.

01

Digital privacy

Use unique passwords, multi-factor authentication and privacy controls. Be deliberate about what personal information is public.

02

Travel awareness

Know your route, keep a charged phone when possible, share plans with someone you trust when appropriate and identify safe places along the way.

03

Boundaries

You do not owe strangers access to your time, location or personal information. Clear, firm boundaries are a practical safety habit.

04

Emergency planning

Keep key contacts available and know the emergency services relevant to your location. A simple plan is easier to use under stress.

Before you leave

A five-point everyday check.

Think through the basics: phone charge, transport or route, essential contacts, payment or backup option, and a clear plan for getting help if something feels wrong.

Digital safety

Protect the information that protects you

Online safety is part of everyday personal safety. Focus on account security, privacy and scam awareness.

Secure your accounts

Use a password manager or unique passwords, enable MFA where available and keep recovery methods current.

Review location sharing

Check app permissions and social settings regularly. Avoid sharing live location or detailed routines publicly when there is no need.

Pause before clicking

Unexpected payment requests, login links or urgent messages deserve verification through a trusted channel before you act.

Protect private files

Store sensitive documents carefully, use device locks and avoid sending personal information to people or services you cannot verify.

When something feels wrong

Trust the signal and create distance.

Move toward safety

When practical, move toward a public or staffed place, contact someone you trust and avoid escalating a confrontation just to prove a point.

Use a simple communication plan

A short message such as “I need you to call me now” can be easier to send than a long explanation. Agreeing on a check-in habit with trusted people can also help.

Preserve important information

For scams, harassment or other incidents where documentation may matter, keep relevant messages, dates and screenshots in a secure place. Follow local laws and seek appropriate professional help when needed.

This is general safety awareness, not emergency response, medical, legal or professional security advice. For an immediate threat, contact local emergency services or a trusted person.
